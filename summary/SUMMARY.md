## The experiments

| Track | What | Embeddings |
|---|---|---|
| **Whole-gender originals** | `men-8`, `women-8` | trained on all of that gender's products |
| **Lazy subcat splits** (`*-lazy-embedding`) | per-subcategory models | **reuse** the whole-gender embeddings |
| **Proper subcat splits** (`*-proper-embedding`) | per-subcategory models | **trained within each subcategory** (LoRA, on the RC) |

Subcategories: **men** = Loafers & Slip-Ons (180 train), Fashion Sneakers (172), Oxfords (130);
**women** = Pumps (626), Flats (496), Fashion Sneakers (430). The original train/val split is
preserved throughout (no re-splitting, no leakage).

Every estimate comes from a **DoubleML Partially Linear Regression** (LightGBM nuisance
learners, cross-fitting, SEs clustered by product), reported as the raw **rank**-elasticity and
the **ϑ-converted demand** elasticity (×1/0.6 ≈ 1.667; He & Hollenbeck 2020 / Victor et al.
Remark 1), for both the **text-only** and **text+image** embedding variants.

## Key findings

1. **Within-gender heterogeneity is real.** Fashion sneakers are the most price-elastic
   subcategory in both genders and under both embedding schemes; flats and pumps are the
   least. The subcategory split surfaces variation that the whole-gender model (−0.17 for
   both genders) averages away.

2. **Lazy ≈ proper on the reliable (women) subcategories.** For the larger women subcats
   (430–626 products), the within-subcat ("proper") and whole-gender-reuse ("lazy") demand
   elasticities are the same order of magnitude with the same ranking; proper runs slightly
   less elastic (e.g. pumps −0.102 vs −0.161, fashion sneakers −0.232 vs −0.337). **Practical
   takeaway:** the cheap lazy shortcut is a defensible approximation when a subcategory is
   large enough, and proper is the more principled, slightly more conservative estimate.

3. **Tiny subcategories are unreliable in either scheme.** The men subcategories (130–180
   products) are too small for stable DoubleML with 256 embedding controls — lazy and proper
   diverge sharply, and some estimates are economically implausible (lazy oxfords came out
   *positive*, +0.24; proper pulls it to ≈0). Treat the men-subcat numbers as illustrative,
   not conclusive; the women subcats are the trustworthy comparison.

4. **The ϑ conversion** scales every estimate ~1.67× toward more elastic, directly addressing
   the advisors' "estimates look too inelastic" concern. Both the raw rank-coefficient and the
   converted demand elasticity are reported everywhere.

5. **Images add information unevenly.** text+image vs text-only differs by subcategory and is
   not uniformly more elastic (see `figures/txt_vs_txtimg.png`); for the women subcats the two
   variants are broadly consistent.

---

## Caveats

- **Sample size dominates** the men-subcat estimates (N = 130–180). DoubleML with 256
  embedding controls needs more products for stability; the women subcats (N = 430–626) are
  the reliable ones.
- **Lazy vs proper is not a perfectly clean A/B:** the whole-gender (lazy) embeddings were
  fully fine-tuned, while the within-subcat (proper) embeddings are LoRA-fine-tuned. This is a
  deliberate, far milder compromise than freezing, and full fine-tuning on 130–630 products is
  not viable (see the `00_part3a–d` embedding notebooks for the rationale).
